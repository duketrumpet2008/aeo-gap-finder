# Impostor al Pastor (Pork Shoulder Tacos)

**Source:** Alison Roman, *Dining In* (transcribed from the book — photos provided)
**Serves:** 6 to 10
**Time:** ~1 hr active + marinating (1 hr room temp minimum, up to 24 hrs refrigerated)
**Meal slot:** Thursday dinner

> **Timing note:** This is *not* a long braise — the pork shoulder is sliced into steaks, marinated, then seared in batches (~45–60 min of cooking). Make the marinade and start marinating up to a day ahead.

## Ingredients
- 1 pineapple, skin and core removed, halved lengthwise
- 8 guajillo or New Mexican chiles (about 3 ounces)
- Kosher salt
- 6 chiles de árbol, seeds removed, or 1 tablespoon crushed red pepper flakes
- 8 garlic cloves, chopped
- 1 large white onion, finely chopped
- ⅓ cup apple cider vinegar or white distilled vinegar
- 1 tablespoon ground cumin
- 1 tablespoon ground coriander
- 1 (3- to 4-pound) boneless pork shoulder, sliced into 1-inch-thick steaks
- Freshly ground black pepper
- 2 tablespoons vegetable oil
- 1 cup fresh cilantro, finely chopped
- 4 limes, cut into wedges

**The fixings**
- 30 small corn tortillas, warmed
- 6 radishes, thinly sliced
- 2 tomatillos, finely chopped
- 1 cup cotija or queso fresco cheese, crumbled
- Hot sauce, such as Cholula, Valentina, or Tapatío, for serving
- Sour cream

## Instructions
1. Chop half the pineapple into 1-inch cubes and thinly slice the other half into bite-sized pieces.
2. Toast the guajillo chiles, which you can do in one of three ways: over a live gas flame (using tongs, hold them over a high flame, almost like you're toasting a marshmallow), in a cast-iron skillet over medium-high heat (toast, shaking the skillet frequently, for about 5 minutes), or in a preheated 375°F oven on a baking sheet (just let them do their thing for 8 to 10 minutes). You want the chiles to be puffed and lightly charred, not burned, and any of these methods will get you there with equal results.
3. Once the guajillo chiles are delightfully toasted, use kitchen shears to remove the stem and cut them into rings. Keep the seeds in if you like the extra heat (these chiles aren't that spicy to begin with), but you can discard all or half of the seeds for something more mild.
4. In a medium saucepan, bring 2 cups salted water to a simmer and add the guajillo chiles and chiles de árbol (do not add red pepper flakes, if using, yet). Remove the pan from the heat and let it sit 15 minutes; the chiles should be soft enough that they basically fall apart when rubbed between your fingers.
5. Using a slotted spoon, transfer the chiles to a blender (reserve the soaking water), along with the garlic, half the onion, the vinegar, 2 tablespoons salt, the cumin, coriander, cubed pineapple, and the red pepper flakes (if you're using them). Add about ½ cup of the reserved chile water and purée until you've got a smooth paste, adding more water as needed to help the blender do its job.
6. Season the pork with salt and black pepper and rub the chile-pineapple mixture all over each piece of meat. Place it in a ziplock bag or a bowl and cover. Let sit for at least an hour at room temperature, or up to 24 hours in the refrigerator.
7. When you're ready to go, heat 1 tablespoon of the oil in a large skillet. Working in batches, sear each piece of meat until it's golden brown and bits of the marinade have begun to caramelize, about 4 minutes per side. Transfer the meat to a cutting board and repeat with the remaining oil and pork.
8. Once the meat is cool enough to handle, slice, chop, or dice it into smaller, taco-sized pieces. Return these smaller pieces to the skillet and cook over medium-high heat, tossing to coat in all the deeply caramelized bits on the bottom of the skillet, letting them crisp up a bit for the second time (this is where that LA taco truck magic happens).
9. Meanwhile, combine the thinly sliced pineapple with the chopped cilantro and remaining onion in a small bowl. Season with salt, black pepper, and a squeeze of lime; set aside.
10. Serve the meat with warm tortillas (even better if you drag them through the fat in the skillet first), sliced radishes, cotija, tomatillos, sour cream, hot sauce, the pineapple salsa, remaining cilantro, and lots of cold, cheap beer.

## Prep-ahead & notes
- **DO AHEAD (from the book):** The marinade can be made 2 days ahead and refrigerated. Pork can be marinated 1 day ahead.
- For this week: make the marinade Wednesday evening and marinate the pork overnight — Thursday dinner is then just searing, crisping, and assembling (~1 hr).
- Leftover cooked pork keeps 3–4 days refrigerated and re-crisps well in a skillet.
