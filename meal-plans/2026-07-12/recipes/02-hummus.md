# Best Hummus
**Source:** [Cookie and Kate (Kathryne Taylor)](https://cookieandkate.com/best-hummus-recipe/)
> ⚠️ Transcribed from memory — the source site was unreachable from this environment. Verify quantities against the link above before shopping/cooking.
**Serves:** Makes about 2 cups
**Time:** 10 min prep / 30 min cook / 40 min total
**Meal slot:** Sunday dinner — Greek Feast (leftovers = Monday lunch)

## Ingredients
- 1 can (15 ounces) chickpeas, rinsed and drained, or 1½ cups cooked chickpeas
- ½ teaspoon baking soda (if using canned chickpeas)
- ¼ cup lemon juice (from 1½ to 2 lemons), more to taste
- 1 medium-to-large clove garlic, roughly chopped
- ½ teaspoon fine sea salt, to taste
- ½ cup tahini
- 2 to 4 tablespoons ice water, more as needed
- ½ teaspoon ground cumin
- 1 tablespoon extra-virgin olive oil
- Garnishes: drizzle of olive oil, sprinkle of ground sumac or paprika, chopped fresh parsley

## Instructions
1. Place the chickpeas in a medium saucepan and add the baking soda. Cover the chickpeas by several inches of water, then bring the mixture to a boil over high heat. Continue boiling, reducing heat if necessary to prevent overflow, for about 20 minutes, or until the chickpeas look bloated, their skins are falling off, and they're quite soft.
2. In a fine-mesh strainer, drain the chickpeas and run cool water over them for about 30 seconds. Set aside (no need to peel the chickpeas — the baking-soda simmer does the work).
3. Meanwhile, in a food processor or high-powered blender, combine the lemon juice, garlic, and salt. Process until the garlic is very finely chopped, then let the mixture rest so the garlic flavor can mellow, ideally 10 minutes or longer.
4. Add the tahini to the food processor and blend until the mixture is thick and creamy, stopping to scrape down any tahini stuck to the sides and bottom of the processor as necessary.
5. While running the food processor, drizzle in 2 tablespoons of ice water. Scrape down the sides, then blend until the mixture is ultra smooth, pale, and creamy. (If your tahini was extra thick to begin with, you may need to add 1 to 2 tablespoons more ice water.)
6. Add the cumin and the drained, cooled chickpeas to the food processor. While blending, drizzle in the olive oil. Blend until the mixture is super smooth, scraping down the sides as necessary, about 2 minutes. Add more ice water by the tablespoon if necessary to achieve a super-creamy texture.
7. Taste and adjust as needed — add more salt for overall flavor, more lemon juice for extra zing, or more cumin to taste. Blend again until smooth.
8. Scoop the hummus into a serving bowl. Drizzle with olive oil on top and sprinkle with garnishes, if desired.

## Prep-ahead & notes
- Makes ahead beautifully — the flavor actually improves after a few hours in the fridge. Ideal to make Saturday or Sunday morning for the feast.
- **Storage:** leftover hummus keeps well in the refrigerator, covered, for up to 1 week. It also freezes well for up to 1 month; thaw in the fridge and stir well (add a splash of water if it has thickened).
- The two keys to the ultra-creamy texture: simmering canned chickpeas with baking soda until the skins fall off, and blending the tahini with **ice-cold** water, which lightens it to a pale, whipped consistency.
- Resting the lemon-garlic-salt mixture for 10+ minutes mellows the raw garlic bite — don't skip it.
- Serve slightly warm or at room temperature with the fresh pita.
