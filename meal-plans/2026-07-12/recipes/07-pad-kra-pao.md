# Pad Kra Pao (Thai Basil Pork Stir-Fry with Holy Basil)

**Source:** [The Woks of Life](https://thewoksoflife.com/pad-kra-pao/)
> ⚠️ Transcribed from memory — the source site was unreachable from this environment. Verify quantities against the link above before shopping/cooking.

**Serves:** 4 (with rice)
**Time:** ~10 min prep / ~10 min cook / ~20 min total (approximate)
**Meal slot:** Thursday lunch

## Ingredients

**Stir-fry** (quantities are my best recollection — medium confidence)
- 3 tbsp neutral oil (vegetable, canola, or avocado)
- 3 shallots, thinly sliced
- 5 cloves garlic, sliced or roughly chopped
- 3–6 Thai bird's eye chilies, deseeded (if you like) and thinly sliced — adjust to your heat tolerance
- 1 lb (450 g) ground pork (ground chicken, turkey, or beef also work)
- 2 tsp sugar (light brown or granulated)
- 2 tbsp fish sauce
- 1 tbsp soy sauce
- 1 tsp dark soy sauce (for color)
- 2 tbsp oyster sauce
- 1/3 cup low-sodium chicken broth or water (approximate)
- 1 large bunch holy basil leaves (about 1 1/2 cups packed) — Thai basil as a substitute, see notes

**To serve**
- Steamed jasmine rice
- Fried eggs, 1 per person (Thai-style khai dao — crispy-edged, runny yolk), optional but traditional
- Extra fish sauce with sliced chilies (prik nam pla), optional

## Instructions

1. Prep everything before you start: slice the shallots, garlic, and chilies, pick the basil leaves, and mix or measure the sauces — the stir-fry itself moves very fast.
2. Heat a wok (or large skillet) over high heat until just smoking. Add the oil, then the shallots and garlic. Stir-fry for about 1–2 minutes until fragrant and just starting to color.
3. Add the chilies and stir-fry for another 30 seconds or so.
4. Add the ground pork, breaking it up with your spatula. Stir-fry over high heat until the pork is cooked through and starting to brown, letting any liquid cook off — you want some caramelization/sear on the meat, so let it sit against the hot wok in intervals rather than stirring constantly.
5. Add the sugar, fish sauce, soy sauce, dark soy sauce, and oyster sauce. Toss to coat the pork evenly in the sauce.
6. Pour in the chicken broth (or water) to deglaze the wok, scraping up any browned bits. Let it bubble for about a minute so the sauce reduces slightly but the mixture stays saucy enough to spoon over rice.
7. Turn off the heat (or keep it very low), add the holy basil, and toss just until the leaves are wilted, about 30 seconds.
8. If serving with fried eggs: heat a few tablespoons of oil in a skillet over medium-high heat and fry the eggs so the whites bubble up and the edges turn brown and crispy while the yolk stays runny.
9. Serve the pork over steamed jasmine rice, topped with a fried egg, with prik nam pla (fish sauce + sliced chilies) on the side if you like.

## Prep-ahead & notes

- **Holy basil vs Thai basil (the source addresses this directly):** authentic pad kra pao uses holy basil (bai kaphrao / tulsi), which has a peppery, clove-like, slightly sharp flavor and fuzzy, jagged-edged leaves. It can be hard to find outside Thai/Southeast Asian markets. Thai basil (purple stems, anise/licorice flavor) is the most common substitute and makes a delicious dish, though technically that version leans toward "pad horapa." In a pinch, even Italian sweet basil works — the dish will just taste different. Use whichever you can find fresh.
- **Prep ahead:** all the aromatics (shallots, garlic, chilies) can be sliced and the sauces measured out earlier in the day; the actual cooking takes about 10 minutes, so this is a great last-minute lunch. Rice can be cooked ahead.
- **Storage:** leftovers keep 3–4 days refrigerated in an airtight container and reheat well in a skillet or microwave; fry eggs fresh when serving.
- **Protein substitutions:** ground chicken is the other classic version (pad kra pao gai); ground turkey or beef, or chopped chicken thighs, also work.
- **Heat level:** the chili quantity is very flexible — the traditional dish is quite spicy; deseed the chilies or use fewer for a milder version.
- Uncertainty flags: sauce ratios (fish sauce / soy / oyster sauce / sugar) and the broth amount are from memory and could differ slightly from the published recipe — verify against the source link before shopping.
